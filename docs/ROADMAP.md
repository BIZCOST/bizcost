# BizCost Roadmap

Purpose: phases, milestone scope, step status, owner actions and deadline-bound open decisions. Other docs cover the rest: product scope (PRODUCT.md), technical design (ARCHITECTURE.md), tables (DATA_MODEL.md) and rationale (DECISIONS.md).

Last updated: 2026-09-27

Status legend: `DONE` · `IN PROGRESS` · `NEXT` · `PLANNED` · `BLOCKED (reason)` · `DEFERRED (by the owner)`

## How we work

- One milestone at a time. The owner approves each milestone's scope ("Proceed") before work starts.
- Do not build future phases early. Before their phase there is no cost engine, unit/inventory/waste calculation, AI, POS API, payroll, full VAT or advanced reporting. Do not create empty stub files for them either (e.g. no WAC file in M1).
- No placeholder screens. Each module manifest has `availability: 'released' | 'planned'`, and nav, tabs and "+" show only the released modules the business has enabled (ARCHITECTURE.md §Modules).
- Web leads. Mobile gets a flow only when its module ships.
- When a step completes: tick it here, update its status and "Last updated". Record new or changed decisions in DECISIONS.md, not here.
- Ask the owner only when a missing decision would cause significant rework.

## Milestone 2 = Phase 2 Costing Core

Goal: a business enters what it buys the way it buys it, and sees what each product really costs: its materials at the weighted average cost plus its share of running costs. Real profit needs sales (Phase 3).

Estimate: **~8–10 weeks solo** (same basis as M1). **Status (2026-09-27): PLANNED; Step 1 DONE as pre-approval groundwork.** M2 starts when the owner signs off M1 and approves this scope (§Open). Step 1 is pure code in `@bizcost/domain` (no tables, API or screens). It was built before that approval as the groundwork that "M1 out of scope" names ("The unit engine in domain is a candidate first task for M2"): it stores no data and releases nothing, and a different owner answer changes only that code and its tests. Everything that stores data waits for the owner decisions O1–O5 (§Open, "Before Phase 2"). Web only: mobile waits for M1 Steps 4 and 8.

Release rule: the M2 modules (Products & Services, Materials, Suppliers, Purchases, Expenses, Running Costs, Files, Cost Engine) stay `planned` until Step 7 releases them together. Until then their screens run only in tests (a test registry, as in M1 Step 7).

- [x] **Step 1: Domain engines** (DONE 2026-09-27; decisions D-107–D-110; pre-approval groundwork, see the status above). Pure TypeScript in `@bizcost/domain`, decimal.js only.
  - Built: decimal kinds checked against their columns at run time, exact products and one rounding, and `parseNumber` for typed input in either language (D-107); the two rounding policies with the ISO-4217 minor-unit table (D-033); document line maths (discount before VAT, per-line rounding, totals as sums of the printed lines); the units engine (six dimensions; packs and cross factors per material; exact fractions; cost per base unit; recipe line cost; D-108); the WAC engine (receipts at what was paid, issues, negative stock, a reversal as if the purchase was never posted, replay of a ledger; D-109).
  - Tests (183 in the package): the owner's examples as golden tests (carton → AED 0.006 per ml → AED 1.20 for 200 ml; 6.666666666667, shown as 6.67; reversing the second purchase gives exactly 6). Fast-check properties: conservation (value = Σ in − Σ out − Σ corrections); zero quantity ⇒ zero value, and positive stock never has a negative value; after receipts the WAC = value ÷ quantity to 12 decimals, and it always lies within the costs of the receipts that still stand; a reversal right after a posting restores the previous state at any stock level; emptying the stock costs exactly its value however the usage is split; conversions are exact and additive. The numeric review's cases are kept as regression tests.
  - Moved out of Step 1:
    - the purchase-line cost (document discount split, delivery split, VAT in or out of cost) → Step 3, because it follows O3's VAT rule;
    - the recipe roll-up with "no price yet" → Steps 4 and 6 (computed in SQL);
    - supplier returns and credit notes → out of M2 (not in the owner's spec);
    - transfers: not a WAC movement (one average per business); transfer screens are Phase 4;
    - "Make Round Amount" → Phase 3 (Quotations & Invoices; D-107).
  - Still waiting: the running-cost allocation function waits for O4, and the recent-purchases average for O5.

#### Before Phase 2: owner decisions in plain words (O1–O5)

**O1 · Customers and suppliers: one list or two?** BizCost records suppliers now, and customers too from Phase 3. Sometimes one company is both (a wholesaler you buy from and also sell to).

- **A)** One contact list: each person or company is saved once and marked "supplier", "customer" or both. The app still shows separate Suppliers and Customers pages.
- **B)** Two separate lists: a company that is both is typed twice, and its details can drift apart.
- Recommendation: **A**. It follows the "enter everything once" rule and costs almost nothing now; going from B to A later means merging records.

**O2 · Names in two languages.** Products, materials, suppliers and running costs need names.

- **A)** One name in any language, plus an optional Arabic name. The Arabic app shows the Arabic name when there is one (the rule the business name already follows, D-097).
- **B)** One name only, in whatever language is typed.
- **C)** Two required names, English and Arabic.
- Recommendation: **A**. Most people type one name; those with Arabic-speaking staff, or Arabic invoice lines later (Phase 3), add the second. C doubles the typing; B makes Arabic invoices harder later.

**O3 · The rules around the average cost (DECISIONS.md P-001).** One average cost per material for the whole business is decided (D-006). The proposed rules can be accepted one by one:

1. A purchase updates the average when it is posted (confirmed), not when it is saved as a draft.
2. A purchase dated in the past changes the average from the day it is posted; past costs stay as they were.
3. A posted purchase is never edited: it is cancelled and a new one is posted. The average goes back to what it would be without the wrong purchase. Costs already recorded stay, and the difference is recorded once.
4. VAT you can reclaim never counts as cost. VAT you can't reclaim (not VAT-registered, a non-tax invoice, no invoice) is part of what you paid. This refines the product rule "VAT never inflates cost" (PRODUCT.md §4 rule 12).
5. Quantities are kept per branch; the cost is one for the whole business.
6. An optional "books closed up to" date stops changes on and before that date.

- **A)** Accept all six.
- **B)** Accept all except the ones you name. For rule 2, the alternative is to recalculate the past whenever an older purchase is added or corrected: past costs and reports change, and it is slower.
- Recommendation: **A**.
- Asked later, with Phase 3 (nothing uses stock up before then): may stock go below zero?

**O4 · How running costs enter each product's cost.** Example: running costs of AED 15,000 a month. Each option shows the share as its own line, next to the materials.

- **A) The same amount on every item sold.** You expect to sell 3,000 items a month, so each item carries AED 5.
  - For: one number you know; simple to explain; works for custom orders and services with no fixed price; it doesn't move with the price, so it helps you set prices; from Phase 3 it uses your real count.
  - Against: a AED 3 water and a AED 40 cake carry the same AED 5 (the water shows a loss).
- **B) A share of the selling price.** You expect AED 60,000 of sales a month, so running costs are 25% of sales: an item sold at AED 20 carries AED 5.
  - For: one number you know; bigger items carry more; from Phase 3 it uses your real sales.
  - Against: an item without a fixed price (custom orders, quotes, services; e.g. the home baker's custom cakes, 3D prints, workshop jobs) gets no share until it is sold; raising a price raises its "cost", so it can't help you set a price.
- **C) By working time.** AED 15,000 ÷ 300 working hours = AED 50 an hour; each product or job carries the cost of the minutes it takes.
  - For: the fairest for workshops, services and 3D printing.
  - Against: it needs the time of each product, and time arrives with Phase 5.
- **D) A share of the material cost.** Running costs ÷ the materials you buy in a month (the average of your last 3 months of purchases, which M2 records; until then, your estimate). AED 15,000 ÷ 30,000 = 50%, so a product with AED 4 of materials carries AED 2.
  - For: no guess once you have purchases; works for custom orders (they have materials); it doesn't move with the price; bigger products carry more.
  - Against: work with few materials (services, handmade work, machine time) carries little, which C covers from Phase 5; months of bulk buying move the rate, hence the 3-month average.
- Recommendation: **D** now, and **C** added for time-based work with Phase 5.
- Proposed with it (the default unless you object): Expenses and Running Costs share one list of categories. Product costs use your regular amounts. From Phase 3, real profit counts each running cost once, at its regular amount: a bill in a running-cost category is its payment, not a second cost, and BizCost shows when the bills run above or below the regular amount (Step 5).

**O5 · The average cost for businesses that don't count their stock (DECISIONS.md P-001).** The average follows the stock on hand, and only stock counts correct that quantity. A business that never counts its stock (e.g. a home baker) never corrects it: purchases pile up in the average forever, so it falls behind prices. Example: flour at AED 5 for a year, then AED 7: the average says 5.15, 26% below the real price. This lasts; it is not only a problem in M2.

- **A)** The same average for everyone. Simple, but for these businesses the cost falls behind prices, and Phase 3 must start with an opening stock count or a reset.
- **B)** Until a business counts its stock (Phase 4, only if it keeps stock), its average is the average of its purchases in the last 90 days. After its first count, the full average applies.
- **C)** The last purchase price until the first count. It follows prices at once, but one unusual purchase swings the cost.
- Recommendation: **B**. It is still one average per material for the whole business, updated by each purchase (D-006). In every case the material page and the cost breakdown also show the last purchase price.
